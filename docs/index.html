Derivation — how the observation became a number

Sources

Zero-spend, public sources only. For this cohort that is the public npm registry document for each package. Nothing behind a login is read. No paid data, no third-party security feeds, no scanners, and no other public grading service is consulted or reproduced.

Layer derivation

Each package’s public metadata is derived into five layer scores on a 1.0 (best) to 5.0 (worst) scale: provenance, mutation, conduct, implementation, stewardship. The layer scores are combined by a published weight vector into a single score, and that score is banded into a grade. This layer derivation is a candidate and is NOT locked (v0.3-rc); it is subject to change before v0.3 is locked.

Eligibility

An entity with fewer than 3 published releases, or fewer than 90 days of registry history, is Not Rated (NR). NR is an eligibility outcome, not a grade, and carries no adverse meaning.

Conclusion — what the grade does and does not mean

What a grade asserts

A Datumline mark is an opinion about publicly observable supply-chain hygiene signals for a named software package, derived by the published method above from the public registry record on the stated date. It is a statement about the record, not about the people who maintain the package.

What a grade does not assert

A grade is not a finding of a vulnerability, a claim that a package is unsafe to run, a claim of wrongdoing by any maintainer or vendor, a certification, an audit, an endorsement, or investment or procurement advice. A grade is point-in-time and can change on the next run without any conduct by the rated party.

Low-band suppression

Grades DL-4 and DL-5 are suppressed from the public feed while the methodology is provisional. An entity in those bands is published as “IN MARKING” pending review. A worst-band result is never published here. “IN MARKING” therefore does not mean a bad grade was found — it means no grade is being published for that entity at this time.

What was NOT evaluated

Absence of a finding is not a finding of absence. Nothing on this page should be read as a statement that a package is free of defects, vulnerabilities, or malicious behaviour. This method looks at a narrow slice of public metadata, and a clean grade means only that this narrow slice raised nothing — it is not a clean bill of health.

  • No runtime, dynamic, or behavioural analysis. No package was installed, executed, sandboxed, fuzzed, or observed in operation.
  • No source code review. The published source of a package was not read, audited, or statically analysed for defects.
  • No vulnerability scanning. No CVE database, advisory feed, SBOM, or dependency scanner was queried. A package with a known published CVE may carry a clean grade here.
  • No transitive dependency analysis. Only the named package’s own registry record was read, not the packages it depends on.
  • No paid or third-party data. No commercial feed, no other public grading service, no private disclosure, no vendor questionnaire.
  • No non-public information. Nothing behind a login, paywall, NDA, or access control was read. No personal data was collected.
  • No assessment of the maintainers or the vendor as people or as a business — not their competence, honesty, security practices, or financial standing.
  • DL-4 and DL-5 are suppressed (see above), so the published feed is not a complete picture of the cohort’s distribution.
  • The layer-derivation mapping itself is provisional and is not locked.
Independence, reply, corrections

Independence

No rated party pays for a grade. Datumline accepts no payment, consideration, or other benefit from any entity in exchange for being rated, for a particular grade, for a change to a grade, for expedited marking, or for removal from the cohort. Rated parties do not select the methodology, the weight vector, the cohort, or the publication schedule.

A paid report yields the same grade as the public feed. Where a report is sold, what is sold is the explanation — the evidence trail, the layer detail, the history — and never the grade itself. The grade in any paid product is byte-identical to the grade in feed.json for the same entity on the same run. If a paid report and the public feed ever disagree, the public feed is correct and the discrepancy is a defect we will publish a correction for.

Right of reply — 5 business days

Any rated party may respond to its mark. We will publish a response received within 5 business days of a mark being published, alongside that entity, at no charge and without requiring agreement to any terms. A response may dispute the facts, the derivation, or the conclusion. We will also publish responses received after 5 business days where practicable; the 5-business-day window is the commitment, not the limit.

Factual corrections raised during the reply window are acted on before anything else. To exercise the right of reply, open a public issue on the project issue tracker, which is the record of contact.

Corrections

Corrections are published in the same location and with equal prominence as the original. A correction to anything on this page appears on this page, at the same level, not in a footnote, a changelog, or a separate page. A correction to a grade in feed.json appears in that feed. Corrections are not silently overwritten: the correction states what was published before, what is published now, and when it changed. We do not remove a mark to resolve a complaint — we correct it, or we publish the reply.

Naming and marks

Entities are named in nominative use only — the name is used to refer to the thing being discussed, which is the only way to report on it.
